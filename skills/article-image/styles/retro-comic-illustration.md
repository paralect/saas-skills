STYLE: a vintage ink cartoon in the manner of 1950s American instructional and advertising illustration, printed on aged paper. A drawn scene with real people in it.

- Background is aged cream paper: warm sepia tone, visible grain, faint stains, a soft darker vignette at the edges. Never pure white.
- Line work is black ink with varied weight, like a brush pen. Confident, slightly loose, clearly drawn by a person. Contours heavier than interior detail.
- Shading is loose grey ink wash in 2 or 3 soft brush shapes: 1 behind the figure standing in for the room, 1 under it as a cast shadow, 1 on the shaded side of the body. Scrubby and unfinished at the edges. It never fills the frame.
- People are semi-realistic with mid-century proportions and expressive faces: combed hair as solid black ink masses, strong eyebrows, visible jaw. Plain period clothing, rolled sleeves, no logos.
- Everything is greyscale except the accent color, on exactly 1 element.
- Nothing later than about 1965 appears in the frame. Every prop is the period equivalent.
- Lettering is bold condensed hand drawn capitals, slightly italic, arched or on a slight tilt, in the same ink weight as the drawing.
